## What the numbers say, and what they do not

**The descriptor-only model is real, and it is weak.** It beats the trivial floor under both
splits by a wide margin — 31.1% and 24.2% RMSE reduction — so the 12 physicochemical
descriptors carry genuine signal about DPP4 pIC50. It also overfits heavily: test RMSE is
2.24x (random) and 2.58x (scaffold) its training RMSE, from a fully grown forest with unit
minimum leaf size. The gap is visible by design; this gate does not try to close it.

**The scaffold split is plainly harder, and the mean predictor tells you how much of that is
the split rather than the model.** The forest's test RMSE rises by 0.154 log units from
random to scaffold allocation. But the mean predictor — which uses no chemistry at all, and
cannot leak anything — also degrades by 0.077 log units across the same two test sets. So
roughly half of the forest's scaffold penalty is already present in a model that sees no
structure, and must come from the two test sets differing in composition and label
distribution, not from the loss of shared training cores. Gate 2 measured the structural
side of this directly: 268 shared exact scaffolds and 120 test compounds with a Tanimoto
≥0.90 training neighbour under random allocation, versus 0 and 44 under scaffold holdout.
The two sources of difficulty are both present and **this design cannot separate them**. The
honest statement is that the contrast is consistent with easier interpolation under random
allocation, partly confounded with a harder, differently distributed scaffold test set. It is
not a causal estimate of a leakage penalty, and the mean-predictor control is the reason I
can put a number on the confound instead of waving at it.

**The Gate 2 leakage probe is now superseded as a floor, but not as evidence.** The fixed
1-nearest-neighbour fingerprint probe scored 0.970 (random) and 1.129 (scaffold) test RMSE.
The descriptor forest edges it under both splits, so the probe is no longer the number Gate 4
must beat — these baselines are. The probe keeps its Gate 2 role as a structural-reuse
diagnostic, which is what it was built for; it was never a calibrated model.

**On repeat dispersion: no figure is adopted here.** The mean predictor's test RMSE under both
splits (1.381, 1.458) exceeds all three Gate 2 statistics. The forest's random test RMSE
(0.951) sits above 0.828 and 0.310 but below 1.011; its scaffold test RMSE (1.104) exceeds all
three. These are descriptive comparisons between point estimates computed on different
populations and different targets — the repeat statistics describe dispersion among
individual reported measurements, while these errors are predictions of compound medians.
None of the three is a validated floor, none is used as a pass/fail criterion, and
**your Gate 4 choice among them is still open and untouched.**

## What this implies for Gate 4

Gate 4's fingerprint or combined model must beat **0.951 random / 1.104 scaffold test RMSE**
under these exact saved splits, not merely beat the mean and not merely beat the probe. If a
2,048-bit Morgan model cannot clear a 12-column descriptor forest, the extra representational
complexity is not earned, and that is a reportable result rather than a failure to hide.

Three cautions carry forward. The overfitting gap says the headroom Gate 4 is chasing is
partly variance, so a tuned model should be compared on the same boundaries with the same
honesty about train/test gaps. The bit-length question is open and must be decided on a
training-only criterion, never by comparing held-out metrics — 1,854 of 3,965 compounds
currently lose at least one substructure identifier to a 2,048-bit collision. And neither
feature block distinguishes stereoisomers while 286 curated compounds share a connectivity
skeleton with another compound, which is a measured ceiling on any model built from these
features.

## Choices to defend in the viva

- **Why report a baseline at all.** Without the mean predictor, an R² of 0.52 looks like an
  achievement rather than a measurement; without the descriptor forest, a fingerprint model's
  score has nothing to be compared against except zero. The floor is what converts a number
  into a claim about added value.
- **Why a trivial mean and not "no model".** `DummyRegressor(strategy="mean")` is the
  minimiser of squared error given no features, so it is the correct floor for RMSE and R²
  specifically. A near-zero negative test R² is the expected result, not a bug.
- **The 12 descriptors.** Lipinski's rule-of-five terms (MolWt, MolLogP, NumLipinskiHBD,
  NumLipinskiHBA) plus Veber's flexibility and polarity terms (NumRotatableBonds, TPSA),
  extended with size (HeavyAtomCount), polarisability (MolMR), ring content (RingCount,
  NumAromaticRings), saturation (FractionCSP3) and polar functionality (NumHeteroatoms).
  Every column has a name a chemist can argue with, which is what makes a later chemical
  interpretation possible. No 3D descriptor: those need conformer generation, a stochastic
  embedding step this gate has no reason to introduce.
- **`NumAliphaticRings` omitted** because RDKit partitions SSSR rings into aromatic and
  aliphatic, making it exactly `RingCount − NumAromaticRings`. The identity was measured
  across all 3,965 compounds (maximum deviation 0.0), not assumed.
- **MolWt and HeavyAtomCount correlate at 0.980/0.982** on training rows only. Nothing was
  dropped on the strength of it; it is reported so an interpretation cannot mistake two
  near-duplicate columns for independent evidence about size.
- **2,048 Morgan bits, radius 2, nonchiral**, inherited unchanged from the Gate 2 similarity
  audit so the audited shared-core and near-neighbour counts describe the real input space.
  Measured, not scored: 9,309 distinct identifiers fold into 2,028 occupied bits and 1,854
  compounds lose at least one. Changing it is a Gate 4 question. **The fingerprints are
  cached but enter neither Gate 3 baseline.**
- **The forest hyperparameters were declared in `RF_SPEC` before the first run** and never
  chosen by a score: 200 trees, fully grown, `max_features=1.0`, `min_samples_leaf=1`,
  bootstrap on, squared-error criterion, `n_jobs=1`, seed 42. More trees reduce Monte Carlo
  variation; a single worker fixes floating-point accumulation order so results are
  byte-reproducible. These are engineering defaults, **not evidence of optimality** — a
  defensible answer to "why these?" is "because they were fixed in advance, and tuning them
  is Gate 4's job."
- **Why there is no scaler or imputer.** Not "we fitted it correctly" but "there is nothing
  fitted to get wrong": a tree ensemble is invariant to monotone per-column rescaling, so a
  scaler would add a fitted object with no benefit. Featurisation is provably stateless —
  each row is a function of one standardised molecule alone. Non-finite values abort the run
  rather than being imputed, so no dataset-level statistic exists that could have leaked.
  The only fitted objects in Gate 3 are the two estimators, fitted on training rows alone.
- **Features come from the standardised structures**, never raw SMILES, and no label is read
  during featurisation. All 3,965 curated compounds featurised with 0 failures; a failure
  would be reported and abort rather than silently shorten the matrix.
- **The splits were reused, never regenerated.** The evaluator reads the saved assignment
  CSVs and verifies their checksums against the Gate 2 audit; a test fails the build if
  baseline code ever calls a split generator.
- **Equal compound weight** regardless of report count, so CHEMBL1422's 71 collapsed reports
  cannot buy extra fitting weight.
